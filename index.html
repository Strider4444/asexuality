Asexuality

What is it, really?

Asexuality is an orientation which describes people experience little to no sexual attraction towards other people. It does not always mean a total lack of attraction, it can also apply to someone only feels limited attraction, or only experiences attraction in certain cases.

Asexuality is not lack of libido, and it is not the choice to avoid sexual relationships.

Asexuality is a spectrum, and everyone will have a different experience and identity within this spectrum!

Key Terms

Split Attraction Model

This is a framework that distinguishes between the different types of attraction that one might feel. It communicates to us that attractions don't always overlap with each other, so an asexual person might experience other attractions, such as homoromantic attraction.

Attraction Types

Romantic: A feeling of infatuation towards someone else, typically with a desire to pursue a romantic relationship.

Sexual: An involuntary desire to be with someone in a sexual manner, or finding someone attractive in a way which might trigger sexual desire.

Platonic: Wanting to be friends with someone, close or otherwise, in a way that doesn't involve romantic or sexual feelings attached.

Desire vs Attraction

Attraction: An involuntary feeling towards someone's qualities, physical or otherwise.

Desire: The wish to pursue actions based on attraction, such as developing a relationship.
